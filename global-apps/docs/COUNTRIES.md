# Country selection and cultural scope

Selection date: 3 October 2026. Rekha already serves the original India context. Ten additional markets were selected from the higher reported rates outside India in Pew Research Center's comparable 2024 survey, published in 2025. The user subsequently requested the United States and United Kingdom as well, giving 12 apps.

The survey question concerns consulting a fortune teller, horoscope **or another way to see the future**. It is broader than astrology and measures reported practice, not a global ranking of belief or market size. It covers 35 countries for this question and excludes many countries entirely. The 10-market choice is an editorial use of that proxy, not a claim that these are the definitive ten most astrology-believing countries.

## Cultural references

- Japan: [Japan National Tourism Organization on omikuji](https://www.japan.travel/en/uk/inspiration/two-bows-and-two-claps-the-ins-and-outs-of-shinto-shrines/). The app explains the distinction; it does not claim to issue a shrine's fortune slip.
- Korea: [Korea.net, Facts About Korea](https://www.korea.net/koreanet/fileDownload?fileUrl=pdfdata%2F2015%2F12%2FFacts-KoreanLife_en_1217.pdf) describes saju in relation to birth time and date. No four-pillars engine is included.
- Singapore: [National Heritage Board's zodiac-themed Lunar New Year campaign](https://www.nhb.gov.sg/what-we-do/our-work/sector-development/museum-roundtable/2026-museum-roundtable-lunar-new-year-hongbao-campaign). This supports one cultural context, not a universal Singaporean belief.
- Nigeria: [UNESCO, Ifá divination system](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ifa-divination-system-00146). The app explicitly avoids presenting Western astrology as Ifá.
- Sri Lanka: [Sri Lanka Tourism festival information](https://www.srilanka.travel/festive) and [New Year description](https://srilanka.travel/index.php?event=381&id=26&route=event%2Fevents). No auspicious time is calculated or prescribed.
- Thailand: [Thai calendar overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_calendar) describes birth-day colours and weekday Buddha imagery. The app offers only a broad cultural note, without prescribing colours, worship or auspicious times.

Other country cards deliberately describe optional journaling and personal choice instead of inventing a nationally uniform or sacred ritual. Modern Western astrology is the theme for the US/UK and several other apps; tarot, Ifá, omikuji, saju, BaZi and feng shui are not treated as interchangeable methods.

Names and marks are new product concepts, not trademark clearance. Ten customer-language dictionaries are included. Nigeria currently uses English; Sri Lanka uses Sinhala and English; Singapore uses Chinese and English. These do not cover every language in those countries. Obtain native-speaker and cultural review before commercial publication.
